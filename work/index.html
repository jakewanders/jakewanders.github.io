OpsDesk

A multi-tenant operations SaaS for small businesses: customers, a sales pipeline board, tasks, quotes with PDF, reports, and CSV import and export. I wrote the product spec, designed the interface, built the full stack and shipped it, with an isolated live demo that anyone can open without signing up.

Isolation lives in the database, not just the UI: every workspace’s rows are separated by Postgres row-level security, and one permission matrix drives both the buttons you see and the rules the server enforces. Accessible by test: pages are checked with axe in light and dark themes, at 375 px and at 200% zoom, with keyboard-only walkthroughs; Lighthouse accessibility scored 100 on all 34 runs. Built with agents, under guardrails: a written spec and a dependency-ordered backlog, one issue at a time, nothing marked done without a recorded command result, tests broken on purpose to prove they fail, and a human decision for anything involving secrets, DNS or publishing.

SiteLaunch RVA

My web studio for Richmond small businesses. Sites go live two weeks after kickoff, sold as a one-time build or with $0 down on a monthly care plan. I set the packages and prices after surveying what Richmond competitors charge and include, then rebuilt the site around them.

One source of truth for pricing: every package, price and term lives in one typed module. It renders the packages page and the contact form, and it also generates the schema.org offers and /llms.txt, so what people, search engines and AI tools read can’t drift apart. AI-search ready: a robots.txt that names the AI crawlers it allows, LocalBusiness structured data, and a sitemap that pulls blog posts from Sanity. Motion that stays cheap: a page-enter transition, a header that condenses on scroll, and a reading-progress bar that writes straight to the DOM from requestAnimationFrame. All of it switches off under prefers-reduced-motion.

EdgeSeeker

An automated trading system for Kalshi prediction markets that runs two strategies side by side. A news-driven pipeline watches 50+ RSS feeds and a dozen Telegram channels, matches stories to open markets, and routes them through a five-agent LLM swarm (Claude, Gemini, GPT) that returns structured trade signals. A separate latency-arbitrage engine sits on Coinbase and Kalshi WebSockets and acts on crypto moves before the market maker reprices.

The interesting engineering is in the guardrails: every market is bucketed by certainty with its own sizing and exit rules; daily spend limits, per-event exposure caps, and drawdown circuit breakers sit in front of execution; and the whole thing is documented as a runbook, an architecture doc, and SQL analytics so it can be operated — and audited — by someone who didn't write it.

Site-launch pipeline

A turnkey pipeline for producing small-business websites at agency scale. Shared packages — Zod content schemas per vertical, an SEO kit (schema.org, Open Graph, sitemaps), an accessibility-first component kit, and CMS config generators — feed per-vertical Next.js templates. A CLI scaffolds a new site; an audit script runs Lighthouse; agents fill content against the schemas. The demo site ships at Lighthouse 99/100/100/100.

Designed around Architecture Decision Records, runbooks, and playbooks from day one, because the point of the system is that it runs without the original author in the loop.
